01 · Who is building it

Engineer first, scientist by training.

Biochemistry for four years, then four years of data platforms. The habit that carried over is that the measurement outranks what I expected.

I'm a Software Engineer III at JPMorganChase, where I build the high-throughput streaming and batch data infrastructure that Consumer & Community Banking runs on. My focus is reusable platforms: declarative source/sink/transform factories, automatic forward-compatible schema evolution, and recursive flattening for arbitrarily nested data. These are the systems that took my team from roughly 2 to 30 production pipelines a month.

Alongside the day job, I build at the intersection of data and applied ML. I write open-source systems for Retrieval-Augmented Generation and semantic schema matching, using hybrid lexical + dense retrieval with late-interaction (ColBERT) reranking. The same engineering rigor I bring to the lakehouse, pointed at retrieval problems.

My foundation is scientific: a B.S. in Biochemistry with a minor in Computer & Information Science from Ohio State. It shows up in what I do with a result I don't like. In MOMENTUM-X, a private research project of mine that trades on signals from language models, my own evaluation found the ensemble consensus signal was anti-predictive. I recorded that and shipped nothing.
